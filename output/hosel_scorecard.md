## 2. Problem Severity & Frequency

**Score: 6/10**  
**Confidence: 70%**

**Concise rationale:**  
Fragmented inventory, difficult product comparison, and concerns about condition and authenticity are credible problems in used golf equipment. Their frequency and economic importance, however, have not been quantified, and buyers already have several alternative solutions.

**Strongest positive evidence:**
- Used clubs have numerous materially important attributes, including loft, shaft, flex, dexterity, condition, and set composition.
- Hosel’s filters directly address the complexity of product discovery.
- The sizeable golfer population and established used-equipment channels indicate recurring category activity.

**Strongest negative / contrary evidence:**
- No evidence quantifies search time, purchase abandonment, failed transactions, or trust-related losses.
- Buyers can use eBay, specialist retailers, local shops, and golf communities.
- The problem may primarily affect high-intent buyers rather than golfers generally.

**Most important missing evidence:**
- Customer research demonstrating frequency, severity, and willingness to pay.
- Search-to-purchase and abandonment data.
- Evidence of counterfeit, misdescription, stale inventory, or condition-related incidents.
- Repeat used-equipment purchase frequency.

## 10. Regulatory / Execution Risk

**Score: 4/10**  
**Confidence: 78%**

**Concise rationale:**  
Hosel operates in a category with manageable but meaningful marketplace, consumer-protection, VAT, seller-reporting, product-safety, data, and cross-border obligations. The principal risk is not necessarily regulation itself, but the unresolved allocation of legal and operational responsibility across Hosel and its sellers.

**Strongest positive evidence:**
- An asset-light model could limit direct inventory, fulfillment, and product-liability exposure if Hosel remains a referral layer.
- The company appears to recognize trust, authentication, and seller quality as important issues.
- UK regulatory requirements are identifiable and potentially addressable with appropriate contracts and controls.

**Strongest negative / contrary evidence:**
- Hosel’s legal role—affiliate, agent, marketplace, or merchant of record—is unclear.
- No public evidence of authentication standards, seller audits, counterfeit controls, or incident management.
- Stale or inaccurate listings could produce failed orders, disputes, and reputational damage.
- U.S. expansion would add sales-tax, consumer-protection, privacy, product-liability, and marketplace obligations.
- The public terms page was not substantively verifiable in the reviewed evidence.

**Most important missing evidence:**
- Full terms, seller agreements, and partner contracts.
- Merchant-of-record, payment, VAT, HMRC, refund, warranty, and chargeback arrangements.
- Seller verification, counterfeit, fraud, product-safety, and listing-quality procedures.
- Data-protection, cybersecurity, insurance, and liability-allocation documentation.
- Operational service-level agreements with inventory partners.
